Why this has to exist

Your booking site will never tell you the price dropped.

Why would it? You've already paid. That refundable rate you chose is the one piece of leverage you have — a free option to change your mind — and it expires quietly, on a date nobody puts in front of you. Checking it yourself means about 45 visits over three weeks, at the right times, and remembering the deadline. Nobody does that. So the option goes unused, every time.

Timing is everything

A price drop after your deadline is worthless.

So we don't check on a lazy weekly timer — every check is clocked against your cancellation date, and we check hardest in the last three days, when a change still leaves you time to act and a missed one costs you the whole option. You always get a message before the window shuts, whether or not anything moved. Nobody finds out too late.

The obvious questions.

What if the price never drops?

Then you've lost one forwarded email. You'll still hear from us before your free-cancellation window closes, so you're never the person who finds out too late. And you can cancel us any month, no notice.

Can you touch my booking or my card?

No — and we never ask for card details. We can't book, change or cancel anything. We read a forwarded confirmation and send you email. Every decision stays yours.

Why can't I just check myself?

You can. It's roughly 45 checks across three weeks, timed so the last ones land while you can still cancel free. Miss the window and the price no longer matters. That's the bit we do.
