Why this has to exist

Your booking site will never tell you the price dropped.

Why would it? You've already paid. So the extra you spent on a refundable rate — your one piece of leverage — expires quietly, on a date nobody puts in front of you. So the option almost everyone pays for goes unused, almost every time.

The obvious questions.

Is switching rates even allowed?

Yes. You're making a normal new booking and cancelling the old one within the policy you already paid for. Hotels publish both rates knowing this — the flexible premium is priced for people who never act on it. You're just not one of them anymore.

What if nothing ever drops?

Play 2 doesn't need a drop — once plans are certain, the locked-in rate is usually the cheaper one. And in the worst case you got every deadline warning and risked €29, not a missed window on a €400 stay.

Can you touch my booking or card?

No. No card details, no access to your reservation, no bookings made in your name. If you ever want out, one click cancels the subscription and deleting your account is one email.

Why can't I just check myself?

You can — it's dozens of checks over weeks, timed so the late ones land while you can still cancel free, plus never forgetting the date. For one trip, maybe. For every trip, forever? That's the €29.

What happens after the beta?

Billing starts with clear notice by email, founding members keep €29/year, and nothing is ever charged silently. Until then, everything is free and the terms say exactly that.

What's the catch?

Honestly: some bookings pay back and some don't, and we can't tell you in advance which is which. What we can promise is that you'll never miss the moment to find out — and that promise we keep on every single booking.
